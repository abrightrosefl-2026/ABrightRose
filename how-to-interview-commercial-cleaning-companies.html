How to Interview Commercial Cleaning Companies

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Use the same questions and the same scorecard for every company.
  • Ask for documents, not just answers: insurance, a sample checklist and references.
  • Decide on scope clarity, quality control and communication, not on the best presentation.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Think of this as a hiring interview. You are hiring a team to care for your building, so a consistent process pays off.

1. Prepare a scorecard

List the criteria that matter, and score each company from 1 to 5.

CriterionWhat good looks like
Scope clarityWritten, specific and complete
Quality controlNamed process and documentation
PeopleTrained, consistent crew
InsuranceVerified and current
CommunicationNamed contact and fast response
PriceItemized and fair

2. Ask the same questions

Keep a short list and ask in the same order. Good starting points are in questions to ask before hiring and during a consultation.

3. Request documents

  • Certificate of insurance
  • Sample checklist for a building like yours
  • Product list and safety data sheets
  • Two references, including one similar to your facility
  • A sample contract

4. Pay attention to how they listen

Strong companies ask you questions, take measurements and note your priorities. Weak ones start with a standard pitch.

5. Check references

Ask each reference:

  • How long have you used them?
  • How do they respond when something is missed?
  • Has the crew been consistent?
  • Would you hire them again?

6. Compare and decide

Sit down with your scorecards. Look at where the companies differ on the factors you care about most. If the top two are close, ask both a final question or two.

Tip: Involve whoever will work with the vendor day to day, such as the office manager. They will notice details you may not.

Finish with the vendor verification checklist.
