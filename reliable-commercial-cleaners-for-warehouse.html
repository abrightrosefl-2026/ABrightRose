How to Find Reliable Commercial Cleaners for Your Warehouse

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Warehouse cleaning needs safety awareness around forklifts, racking and loading areas, plus the right floor equipment.
  • Confirm the vendor has experience with industrial or warehouse sites before you sign.
  • Agree on schedule, access and the scope in writing.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Warehouses are different from offices. Floors are larger and harder, the dust is heavier, and there are forklifts, racking and loading docks. Not every janitorial company is set up for it.

What a warehouse scope often covers

  • Office, break room and restroom areas (the part most janitorial companies do well)
  • Floor sweeping and scrubbing, sometimes with ride-on or walk-behind machines
  • Dock and entry area cleaning
  • Dust control on ledges, racks and fixtures
  • Trash and recycling management
  • Spill response procedures, where agreed

What to look for in a vendor

  • Experience with industrial or warehouse sites. Ask for a reference from a similar facility.
  • Safety training. Crews should understand your site rules, PPE requirements and traffic patterns.
  • Equipment. Do they own or have access to the floor machines the job needs?
  • Insurance. Confirm coverage fits the risk of a larger industrial site. See commercial cleaning insurance explained.
  • Scheduling. Cleaning around shifts, deliveries and equipment movement
  • Chemical management. Safety data sheets, labeling and compatibility with your floors and any existing site policies

Questions to ask

  1. Have you cleaned warehouses of this size and type?
  2. How do you coordinate around forklifts and operations?
  3. What training do your crews get for our site rules?
  4. Is floor scrubbing included, and what equipment do you use?
  5. How do you document the work?

Agree on the boundaries

Decide which areas the cleaning company handles and which your team handles. Machine cleaning, racking and hazardous material areas are often excluded. Put it in the scope.
