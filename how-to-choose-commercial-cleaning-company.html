How to Choose the Right Commercial Cleaning Company for Your Business

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Define your scope first, then ask every bidder to quote the same scope.
  • Verify insurance and references before price becomes the deciding factor.
  • Choose the company that is clear, documented and responsive, not just the cheapest.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Choosing a commercial cleaning company does not need to be complicated. A clear process saves time and reduces the chance of switching vendors in six months.

Step 1: Define what you need

Write down your square footage, restroom count, floor types, how often you want service, and any special needs. Medical offices, daycares and HOA clubhouses have specific requirements. Mention them early.

Step 2: Decide what matters most

Is it price, flexibility on hours, eco-friendly products, documentation, or industry experience? Knowing your priority makes the comparison easier.

Step 3: Build a short list

Ask other local business owners who they use. Check that each company works with your facility type. A company that cleans medical offices every day understands your needs differently than one that mostly cleans retail.

Step 4: Request walkthroughs and written quotes

Ask each company to visit the building, then to send a written, itemized quote for the same scope. See how to evaluate bids.

Step 5: Verify the basics

  • Certificate of insurance, sent by the insurance agent when possible
  • Business registration and local licensing, see how to verify credentials
  • Two references, including one from a similar building

Step 6: Compare more than price

  • Written scope with task frequency
  • Named point of contact and response time
  • Quality-control method (inspections, checklists, scores)
  • Training and screening practices
  • Products and safety data sheets
  • Contract length and exit terms

Step 7: Start with a clear agreement

A good start includes a written scope, a first-30-days review, and an agreed way to report issues. Read what a contract should include.

A note on fit: The best company for a warehouse may not be the best company for a medical practice. Ask whether your facility type is something they do every day.
