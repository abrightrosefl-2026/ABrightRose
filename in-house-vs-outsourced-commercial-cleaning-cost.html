In-House vs. Outsourced Commercial Cleaning: A Cost Comparison

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Compare the full cost of an employee, not just the hourly wage.
  • Outsourcing includes supervision, supplies, equipment, insurance and backup coverage.
  • The right answer depends on building size, hours and how much management time you can spare.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

The wage on a job posting is not the cost of an in-house cleaner. Compare the full picture.

What an in-house cleaner really costs

  • Wages and any overtime
  • Employer payroll taxes
  • Workers' compensation and general liability insurance
  • Paid time off, holidays and any benefits you offer
  • Supplies, chemicals and equipment, plus repairs and replacements
  • Training and supervision time
  • Hiring, onboarding and turnover costs
  • Cover when someone is sick or leaves

What outsourcing includes

A commercial cleaning company's price usually covers labor, supervision, supplies, equipment, insurance and replacement staff. You pay one invoice, and the company carries the employer costs.

When in-house can make sense

  • A large building that needs cleaning all day
  • Specialized needs and a management team ready to supervise
  • Strong internal HR and training

When outsourcing often fits better

  • Small and mid-sized spaces
  • Evening or weekend cleaning
  • No time to manage staff or buy supplies
  • A need for insurance, backup crews and documented checklists

How to decide

  1. Add up your true in-house cost using the list above.
  2. Get two or three written quotes with the same scope.
  3. Compare cost, risk and the time your own team would spend.
