Signs Your Commercial Cleaning Company Isn't Doing a Good Job

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Look for repeated missed tasks, dirty restrooms, empty dispensers, odors and constantly changing crews.
  • Document what you see with dates and photos, then raise it in writing.
  • If the pattern continues after a fair chance to fix it, use your contract's exit terms.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Even good cleaning companies have bad weeks. The question is whether you are seeing a one-time problem or a pattern.

Signs that deserve attention

  • Restrooms that smell, have dirty fixtures, or run out of supplies
  • Trash that is not emptied or liners that are not replaced
  • Dust building up on ledges, vents and desks that should be done weekly
  • Floors with streaks, sticky spots or visible debris in corners
  • Glass and entry doors with fingerprints that stay for days
  • Break room counters and sinks that are not clean
  • Staff complaints about odors or allergies
  • A different crew every visit, or crews who cannot say what is in the scope
  • Slow or no response when you report an issue
  • Supplies running low without anyone telling you

How to check without guessing

  1. Compare the work to the written scope and checklist.
  2. Do a quick walk-through at the same time each week, such as Monday morning.
  3. Take dated photos of anything you want fixed.
  4. Look at the sign-in or checklist if the vendor provides one.

What to do

  1. Send a written note. Keep it factual: what, where and when.
  2. Ask for a fix by a date. Reasonable vendors will re-clean quickly.
  3. Ask for a root cause. Was it training, staffing or scope confusion?
  4. Request a scheduled review. A 15-minute call every month prevents drift.
  5. Decide after a fair chance. If the same problems come back, it may be time to move on. See how to switch companies.
Fair is fair: Sometimes the scope is the problem. If you expect something that is not written down, discuss adding it. See what to do when service falls short.

A good vendor wants to hear about problems early. If a vendor makes it hard to report them, that is a signal too.
