Restroom Cleaning Standards for Commercial Spaces

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Restrooms are the highest-risk and highest-visibility area in any building.
  • A good routine follows a set order, uses color-coded tools, disinfects touchpoints with correct contact time, and restocks every visit.
  • Frequency should match traffic. Busy restrooms may need checks during the day.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

People judge a business by its restrooms. They are also where germs spread most easily, so the routine matters.

Frequency by use

  • Low traffic (small office): daily
  • Moderate traffic: daily, with a midday check
  • High traffic (medical, daycare, hospitality, events): multiple times per day, with a documented check log

The order of work

  1. Prepare. Put out signs, ventilate, put on gloves and eye protection.
  2. Empty trash and replace liners.
  3. Apply disinfectant to fixtures and high-touch points. Keep surfaces wet for the label's contact time.
  4. Clean top to bottom. Mirrors, dispensers, counters and sinks first.
  5. Toilets and urinals last. Use dedicated brushes and cloths.
  6. Restock soap, paper towels and toilet paper.
  7. Floors last. Sweep or dust mop, then damp mop with the correct dilution, working toward the door.
  8. Check. Look from the doorway: streak-free mirrors, full dispensers, no odors, dry floor.

Our disinfection basics guide explains why you clean first and then disinfect.

Key standards

  • Color-coded cloths and mops used only in restrooms
  • EPA-registered disinfectant used per the label
  • Correct dilution, not "more is better"
  • Wet floor signs until the floor is dry
  • Fixtures, grout and drains detailed weekly or monthly
  • Odor addressed at the source, not masked
  • Reports of leaks, broken fixtures and low supplies

Common problems and causes

ProblemLikely cause
Persistent odorDrains, urinal traps or grout not cleaned
Streaky mirrorsDirty cloth or too much product
Slippery floorsOver-dilution or residue
Empty dispensersRestocking not in scope or supplies short

How to inspect

Use a checklist and look at fixtures at eye level and at floor level. Check behind the toilet and around the base. Compare to your written scope.

Medical offices and daycares often require more frequent disinfection and specific logs. See medical office cleaning and daycare cleaning.
