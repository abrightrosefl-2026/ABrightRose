Can You Clean Everything With Vinegar? Surfaces to Avoid

Published by the ABR team · Updated November 2026

Key takeaways
  • No. Vinegar is acidic and works well on glass and mineral deposits, but it can damage natural stone and some finishes.
  • Never mix vinegar with bleach.
  • For hard water on glass, soak first, then wipe gently. Test a small hidden spot before treating any surface.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

White vinegar has a reputation as the all-purpose green cleaner. It is useful, but it is not safe for everything. Acid is what makes it work, and acid is also what makes it risky on certain surfaces. This matters even more in an office, a lobby or a medical suite, where finishes are expensive.

Where vinegar works

  • Glass and mirrors, where it cuts light film and fingerprints.
  • Mineral deposits and hard water spots on glass, chrome and many faucets and fixtures.
  • Coffee makers and kettles, where it can descale mineral buildup (follow the manufacturer's instructions).

Where to avoid it

  • Natural stone such as marble, limestone, travertine and granite. Acid can dull and etch the surface and can weaken sealants. Use a cleaner made for stone.
  • Hardwood and many sealed floors. Acid can dull or strip some finishes.
  • Grout and sealed surfaces that the manufacturer says to keep acid-free.
  • Anything mixed with bleach or ammonia products. See chemicals you should never mix.

When in doubt, check the product or flooring manufacturer's care instructions and test a small, hidden area first. Commercial finishes often have their own approved cleaners.

Hard water stains on glass

Hard water buildup is made of minerals and does not lift with ordinary soap. A common approach on glass is a spray of equal parts white vinegar and dish soap. The usual method:

  1. Spray the glass and let it sit for 15 to 20 minutes so the acid can loosen the minerals.
  2. Gently scrub with a non-scratching pad or soft brush.
  3. Rinse well and dry with a clean microfiber cloth.

Keep it off metal trim, stone and grout around the glass, and rinse thoroughly afterward. Heavily etched or baked-on glass may need a professional glass restoration product or service.

For storefront and office glass on a schedule, see commercial window cleaning standards and costs.

Vinegar is not a disinfectant

Vinegar can clean, but it is not an EPA-registered disinfectant. Where you need to disinfect, such as restrooms, break rooms and medical areas, use an approved product and follow its contact time. See cleaning vs. disinfecting.

The professional approach

Crews match the product to the surface: neutral cleaners for stone, glass cleaner for glass, descalers where minerals are the problem, and disinfectants only where needed. That is how finishes last, and why a written product list is worth asking for.
