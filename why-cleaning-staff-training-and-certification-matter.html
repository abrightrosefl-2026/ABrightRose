Why Training and Certification Matter in a Commercial Cleaning Staff

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Training is what keeps cleaning consistent, safe and effective, whoever shows up that night.
  • Ask what training new staff get, how it is documented and how often it repeats.
  • Industry programs and product certifications are useful signals, but written training records matter most.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Two cleaners can use the same product and get very different results. The difference is training.

Why training matters

  • Safety. Workers handle chemicals, wet floors and equipment. Proper training reduces injuries and chemical mistakes. OSHA's Hazard Communication standard requires employers to train workers on the hazardous chemicals they use.
  • Infection control. Cleaning, sanitizing and disinfecting are different jobs. Staff need to know contact time, color-coded cloths and the correct order of work.
  • Consistency. A written standard that every worker is trained on means your building is cleaned the same way every visit.
  • Fewer complaints. Trained staff miss less, and they know when to report a problem rather than guess.

What good training covers

  1. Reading product labels and safety data sheets, and never mixing chemicals.
  2. Protective equipment: gloves, eye protection and non-slip shoes.
  3. Cleaning order: top to bottom, clean to dirty, restrooms last.
  4. Floor care, including wet floor signs and correct dilution.
  5. Restroom procedures and color-coded tools.
  6. Site rules: privacy, keys, alarm codes and how to report problems.

Certifications and programs you may see

Some companies hold or follow industry programs, such as those from ISSA and its Cleaning Management Institute, or use products certified by Green Seal or EPA Safer Choice. These are helpful signals. Ask which certification the company actually holds, who it applies to and whether it is current. Training records are often more telling than a logo.

Ask these questions

  • What training does a new cleaner get before working alone at our site?
  • Is training documented, and can we see a sample record?
  • How often is training refreshed?
  • Who supervises and inspects the crew?
Written training records, trained supervisors and regular inspections are the best sign that a company will keep its standard.
