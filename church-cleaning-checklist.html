Church Cleaning Checklist: Sanctuary, Nursery, Hall and Restrooms

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Clean by area and by frequency: daily, weekly, monthly and yearly tasks are different.
  • Nurseries, restrooms and kitchens need the most attention; sanctuaries need the right products for wood and carpet.
  • In South Florida, plan for humidity, hurricane season and extra cleaning around big events.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Houses of worship are used differently from offices. A building might be quiet for days, then host hundreds of people, a nursery full of toddlers, a wedding and a potluck in the same week. A good cleaning plan matches that rhythm.

This checklist works for churches, synagogues, mosques and temples. Adjust it to your own house rules, such as shoe policies and areas that should not be touched.

Daily and after each service

  • Vacuum or sweep the sanctuary and entrances.
  • Wipe high-touch points: door handles, handrails, light switches and push plates.
  • Clean and restock restrooms: soap, paper towels and toilet paper.
  • Empty trash and replace liners.
  • Spot-clean glass doors and entry glass.
  • Reset the nursery and wipe tables and surfaces.

Weekly

  • Mop hard floors and vacuum carpet, including under and between pews or chairs.
  • Dust pew tops, ledges, windowsills and rails with a dry or lightly damp microfiber cloth.
  • Deep-clean restroom fixtures, floors and partitions.
  • Wipe kitchen counters, sinks and appliance exteriors.
  • Disinfect water fountains and coffee stations.
  • Treat carpet stains promptly, before they set.

Monthly

  • Clean vents and ceiling fans.
  • Clean restroom grout and tile.
  • Dust light fixtures and high shelves.
  • Clean behind and under seating and furniture.
  • Wash cloth items that can be washed, following the care instructions.
  • Deep-clean the kitchen: inside appliances, floors and drains.

Once or twice a year

  • Professional carpet extraction in the sanctuary and halls.
  • Window washing, inside and out.
  • Floor stripping and refinishing where needed.
  • Pressure-washing walkways and entrances.
  • Cleaning gutters and drains.

Area-by-area notes

AreaWhat to know
Sanctuary and pewsUse products that are safe for finished wood and upholstery. Do not soak wood. Avoid moving or cleaning sacred or special items unless you have given instructions.
Nursery and children's roomsUse child-safe, non-toxic products. Disinfect toys and tables according to the product label and contact time, and wash soft items regularly.
RestroomsHighest priority. Disinfect fixtures, keep supplies stocked and use separate, color-coded cloths. See restroom cleaning standards.
Fellowship hall and kitchenClean and sanitize after every event. If you serve food to the public, ask your local health department what rules apply.
EntrancesMats, glass and floors take the most wear. Clean them most often, especially in rainy season.
Offices and classroomsTreat them like any office. See the daily cleaning checklist.

Remember the order: clean first, then disinfect, and let disinfectant stay wet for the full contact time. Cleaning vs. disinfecting explains why.

South Florida: humidity, storms and big events

  • Humidity. Constant moisture means carpets, upholstery and closets can develop musty smells and mold. Run air conditioning consistently, report leaks quickly and dry wet areas fast. See humidity and mold in South Florida buildings.
  • Hurricane season. It runs June 1 to November 30. The publication Church Law & Tax summarizes FEMA-based advice for congregations: clear debris from gutters, drains and the area around the building, anchor bookshelves and large cabinets, secure heavy equipment and anything that could blow around, and gather vital records and insurance information. Only do these things when it is safe, and follow evacuation orders.
  • After a storm. Expect water, debris and possible mold. Photograph damage, call your insurer, and get help from a cleaning or restoration professional before reopening rooms that got wet.
  • Holidays and big events. Plan extra cleaning before and after Easter, Christmas, High Holy Days, Ramadan gatherings, weddings and funerals.

Volunteers or a professional?

Many congregations rely on volunteers, which can work well for light daily tasks. Professionals are usually a better fit for restrooms, floors, carpet, kitchens and any task that needs insurance, training or equipment. A mix often works best: volunteers keep the space tidy day to day, and a professional crew handles the deeper work on a schedule.

If you hire a company, ask:

  1. Do you carry insurance, and can you provide a certificate? See commercial cleaning insurance explained.
  2. Which products will you use on our wood, carpet and finishes?
  3. How will you work around our service and event schedule?
  4. Who has keys and alarm codes, and how are they protected?
  5. How do you document each visit and report problems?
