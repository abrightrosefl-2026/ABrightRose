What Should a Commercial Kitchen Deep Clean Include?

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • A real deep clean goes behind, under and inside equipment, not just surfaces.
  • Hood and duct systems require a certified contractor and a service report.
  • Agree on the checklist, access, downtime and chemical safety before the work starts.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

A deep clean is different from a nightly clean. It is the work that happens occasionally to reset the kitchen. Here is a checklist you can use to define the scope with any provider.

Equipment

  • Fryers: drain, boil out or clean the vat, and degrease the exterior
  • Grills, griddles and flat-tops: scrape, degrease and clean grease channels
  • Ovens and ranges: interiors, racks, burners and drip trays
  • Steam tables, warmers and holding equipment
  • Slicers, mixers and prep equipment (disassembled per manufacturer instructions)
  • Ice machines (follow manufacturer's cleaning and sanitizing procedure)
  • Refrigeration: shelves, gaskets, door seals and interior surfaces

Surfaces and structure

  • Walls and backsplashes
  • Ceilings and light fixtures
  • Shelving and storage racks
  • Behind and under equipment
  • Door handles and switches
  • Exhaust hood exterior and filters (hood and duct interior by a certified contractor)

Floors and drains

  • Degrease and scrub floors, including corners and edges
  • Clean floor drains and sinks
  • Clean floor mats
  • Check for grease trap service dates (usually separate from cleaning)

Safety and compliance

  • Confirm that chemicals used are safe for food-contact surfaces where required, and that surfaces are rinsed and sanitized as the product label says.
  • Confirm that the crew has protective equipment and has been trained on the chemicals used. See OSHA's Hazard Communication standard.
  • Ask for a written report and photos before and after.

What to settle before the crew arrives

  1. Scope. Which equipment is included and which is not?
  2. Access and downtime. Will the kitchen be closed? For how long?
  3. Who handles hood and duct work? NFPA 96 requires trained contractors.
  4. Pre-opening inspection. Who confirms the kitchen is ready to cook again?
  5. Insurance. Request a certificate before work starts.
Tip: Photograph problem areas before the deep clean. It gives you a baseline and helps you verify the work.
