What to Do When Commercial Cleaning Service Falls Short

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Document with dates and photos, and report issues in writing.
  • Ask for a fix by a specific date and a root cause.
  • If problems repeat after a fair chance, use your contract's exit process.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Service problems are frustrating. A structured response gets better results than an angry email.

Step 1: Document

Write down what you found, where, and when. Take dated photos. Note whether the item is in the written scope.

Step 2: Report quickly and in writing

Send a short, factual message to your point of contact.

Example: "On Tuesday morning the second-floor restroom had empty dispensers and a full trash can. The checklist lists both as nightly tasks. Please re-clean today and let me know what happened."

Step 3: Ask for a fix and a cause

You want two answers: how and when will it be fixed, and why did it happen? Common causes are staffing changes, unclear scope and supply shortages.

Step 4: Give a fair window

Most reasonable vendors fix issues within one business day. If your contract has a re-clean clause, refer to it.

Step 5: Escalate if needed

If the same issue repeats, ask for a call with a supervisor or manager. Share your list of dated issues. Ask for a written corrective plan.

Step 6: Hold a review

Request a short monthly review with your point of contact for 2 or 3 months. Use the checklist as the agenda.

Step 7: Decide

Consider moving on if:

  • The same problems return after fixes
  • Communication stops or becomes defensive
  • Crews change constantly
  • Safety or security issues arise

Check your contract for notice and termination terms before you act. See how to transition to a new company.

Check your own side

  • Is the problem in the written scope?
  • Did you tell the vendor about it?
  • Have access, alarms or supplies caused delays?
  • Has the building changed (more people, new equipment)?
Best case: A good vendor treats a complaint as a chance to prove itself. How they respond is useful information.
