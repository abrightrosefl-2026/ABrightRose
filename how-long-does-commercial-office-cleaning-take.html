How Long Does Commercial Office Cleaning Take?

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Visit length depends on size, restroom count, floor type, clutter and scope.
  • Ask how many people and how many labor hours the quote assumes.
  • Crew size changes the clock time, not the total labor hours.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

A common question is, "How long will the crew be here?" The honest answer is that it depends, but you can estimate it.

What changes the time

  • Size and layout. Open space cleans faster than many small offices.
  • Restrooms. They take longer than any other area.
  • Floor types. Carpet is quick to vacuum. Hard floors need mopping and drying time.
  • Clutter. Desks piled with papers slow dusting and trash collection.
  • Scope. Disinfecting high-touch points and detailing take extra time.
  • Crew size. Two people finish in half the clock time of one, but use the same labor hours.
  • Frequency. A space cleaned nightly stays easier to maintain than one cleaned weekly.

A rough planning rule

For ordinary office areas, a crew often covers on the order of 2,000 to 4,000 square feet per labor hour, and restrooms take additional time. This is a rule of thumb for planning, not a quote. Professional references, such as ISSA's published cleaning times, give task-level guidance. Your walkthrough will refine the estimate.

An illustrative example

Imagine a 6,000 square foot office with two restrooms and a break room. If general areas take about 2 labor hours, and restrooms and the break room take about 1 more, the nightly visit is around 3 labor hours. One person needs about 3 hours on the clock. A crew of two needs about 1.5 hours. These numbers are illustrative only.

Why this matters

  • Planning around the crew. You will know when the building is empty or occupied.
  • Checking the quote. If the quoted labor hours are far below the estimate, the work may be rushed.
  • Security. Shorter visits mean fewer hours of alarm and access exposure.

Questions to ask

  1. How many people will come and for how long?
  2. How many labor hours does the price assume?
  3. What happens if the visit needs more time?
  4. Which tasks are done every visit and which are weekly or monthly?
Beware of crews that finish suspiciously fast. Compare the time on site to the scope and checklist.
