Germiest Spots in an Office Break Room

Published by the ABR team · Updated November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Wet, food-rich spots, especially sponges and sinks, are often germier than the restroom.
  • Replace sponges often, or switch to cloths that go through a hot wash.
  • Clean appliance filters weekly, run a hot cleaning cycle monthly and leave doors ajar to dry.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Most people assume the restroom is the dirtiest room in the building. In practice, the break room often competes with it. Food, moisture and shared use give bacteria and mold everything they need.

Why wet, food-rich spots lead

  • Sponges and dish brushes. A German study published in *Scientific Reports* looked at used kitchen sponges and found hundreds of kinds of bacteria, including some that can cause infections. Microwaving did not reliably fix the problem, and some bacteria came back. The researchers recommended replacing sponges about weekly.
  • Sinks and drains. Constant moisture plus food particles.
  • Shared handles and buttons. Refrigerator, microwave, coffee machine and faucet handles are touched all day.
  • Appliances. Dishwashers, coffee makers and ice machines trap moisture and residue.

What to do about sponges

  • Replace them every 1 to 2 weeks in an office, sooner if they smell or look worn.
  • Better yet, use washable cloths or dish brushes and run them through a hot laundry cycle.
  • Let them dry between uses; never leave them sitting in a wet sink.

A simple break-room schedule

FrequencyTask
DailyClean and disinfect sink, faucet, counters, table, and fridge, microwave and coffee-machine handles. Empty trash.
WeeklyClean appliance filters, wipe the inside of the microwave and the exterior of appliances, toss old food from the fridge.
MonthlyRun a hot, empty cycle in the dishwasher with a machine cleaner or the manufacturer's recommended method. Descale the coffee maker. Deep-clean the fridge.

Prop dishwasher and washer doors slightly open after use so they can dry.

Smelly dishwasher or washing machine?

Soap scum, hair and food particles trap water and grow mold. The fix: clean the filter weekly, run an empty hot cycle with a dedicated cleaner (or white vinegar if the manufacturer allows), and leave the door ajar. Never mix vinegar with bleach. See chemicals you should never mix.

Clean, then disinfect

Wipe away food and grease first, then disinfect high-touch points and let the product stay wet for its full contact time. Disinfection basics explains the steps.

Source: AARP summary of the Furtwangen University sponge study (Scientific Reports, 2017). The cleaning schedule above reflects general professional practice; follow your appliance manufacturer's instructions.
