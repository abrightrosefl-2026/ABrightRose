How to evaluate a commercial cleaning company before you sign

Published by the ABR team · Updated June 2026

Yes — we're a cleaning company telling you how to vet cleaning companies. We're publishing this anyway, because the questions below are the ones a well-run vendor wants to be asked, and the ones a poorly run one hopes you skip. Use them on us. Use them on everyone.

Before the walkthrough: paperwork that actually matters

  • Certificate of insurance naming your property as additional insured — request it directly from the vendor's insurance agent, not as a PDF from the vendor
  • General liability coverage appropriate to your building's size and use
  • Workers' compensation coverage — if a crew member is hurt in your building and the vendor isn't covered, the liability can land on you
  • Proof the workers are employees or clearly disclosed subcontractors; ask which

The 12 walkthrough questions

  1. Who exactly will clean my building? The same crew nightly, or whoever's available? Consistency is the single biggest quality driver.
  2. How are crew members screened and trained? "We hire experienced people" is not a training program. Ask to see the curriculum or checklist they're trained against.
  3. What does a visit checklist look like? If they can't produce a building-specific checklist, quality depends entirely on memory.
  4. How do you verify the work was done? Initials, inspection scores, photos — anything documented beats "trust us."
  5. Who is my point of contact, and what's the response time? Get a name and a number, not a ticket portal alone.
  6. What happens when something is missed? The honest answer includes a re-clean policy with a timeframe.
  7. What products do you use, and can I see the SDS sheets? Green Seal or EPA Safer Choice certification matters in occupied buildings; safety data sheets should be available without hesitation.
  8. How do you prevent cross-contamination? Listen for color-coded cloths and separate restroom mops. Silence here is a red flag.
  9. What is and isn't in scope? Get the exclusions in writing — interior windows, dishes, high dusting frequency. Most disputes live in the gray area.
  10. How is pricing structured, and what triggers a change? Square footage, frequency, and supply costs should be itemized.
  11. What's your client retention rate? Renewal percentage tells you more than any reference call.
  12. Can I speak to a client of similar building type? A medical office reference means little if you run a warehouse.

Red flags worth walking away from

  • A quote without a walkthrough. Nobody can price your building accurately from a phone call.
  • A price dramatically below the others. Cleaning is labor. A too-low bid means understaffed visits, rushed work, or workers paid off the books.
  • No written scope of work. If it's not written, it's not in scope — and you'll find out at the worst time.
  • Reluctance on insurance verification. Legitimate vendors handle this request weekly. Hesitation means something.
  • Long lock-in with no exit clause. A confident vendor offers a 30-day out after an initial period. Quality should keep you, not a contract.
One honest disclosure: A Bright Rose serves South Florida, and naturally we'd love your business if you're local. But everything above applies anywhere in the country — and a vendor who passes all twelve questions is worth keeping, whoever they are.
