Cleaning Company Background Checks: What Should You Require?

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Ask what screening happens before someone receives keys or alarm codes, and get it in writing.
  • Key control and access logs matter as much as screening.
  • Screening practices must follow the law. Ask your attorney if you need specific requirements.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Cleaning crews often work in empty buildings with access to offices, records and equipment. Asking how a company screens its people is reasonable. This is general information, not legal advice.

What to ask

  1. Do you run criminal background checks before assigning someone to a site? Ask what the check covers and how often it is repeated.
  2. How do you verify identity and work authorization? Employers must verify employment eligibility. In Florida, many private employers are also required to use E-Verify. Ask how the vendor complies.
  3. Do you use employees or subcontractors? Screening may differ. Ask how subcontractors are screened.
  4. Is there a drug-free workplace policy where applicable?
  5. Who is cleared for our building? Ask for a list of crew members assigned to your site and a process to notify you of changes.

Beyond screening: controlling access

Screening is one layer. Access control is another.

  • Unique alarm codes for each person, not shared codes
  • Keys or fobs logged out and returned, with a replacement process if lost
  • Documented clock-in and clock-out at your site
  • Rules against bringing guests
  • A written confidentiality policy, especially for medical offices and daycares
  • Prompt removal of access when someone leaves the company

What is allowed

Background checks are regulated by federal and state law, including consent and notice requirements. A company should follow the rules, and you should not ask a vendor to do anything unlawful. If your facility has specific requirements, such as a school or childcare license standard, check them with your licensing agency.

What to put in the contract

  • The vendor's screening practices
  • Notice if a crew member changes
  • Responsibility for lost keys or codes
  • Confidentiality terms
Be fair and specific: Ask every vendor, including us, to describe their screening process in writing so you can compare answers.
