Norovirus Outbreak at a Kansas School: What Facilities Can Learn

Infection ControlOctober 30, 2026·4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Health officials reported 97 cases linked to one elementary school; the director said that is likely an undercount.
  • Officials urged thorough cleaning and disinfection of contaminated surfaces and frequent handwashing with soap and water.
  • Facilities with children or patients should have a clear plan for vomiting and diarrhea incidents.

Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

In October, the Johnson County Department of Health and Environment in Kansas reported 97 norovirus cases linked to Bentwood Elementary School in Overland Park: 95 among students and staff inside the school and two among family members outside it, according to local news coverage. The department’s director said the number is likely an undercount. Two cases were confirmed by lab testing and the rest were considered epidemiologically linked.

What officials recommended

  • Thorough cleaning and disinfection of contaminated surfaces.
  • Frequent handwashing with soap and water.
  • Staying home for at least 48 hours after symptoms stop.

The director also noted that norovirus causes vomiting and diarrhea, is unrelated to influenza, has no vaccine, and is not treated by antibiotics because it is viral. About 2,500 outbreaks are reported nationally each year, a figure he said is also likely an undercount.

Why this matters for daycares, medical offices and schools

Norovirus spreads easily where people share space and touch the same surfaces. A single incident can spread quickly if it is not handled the right way. Facilities that serve children or patients should plan ahead:

  • Have a written incident plan. Know who is called, which areas are closed, and how the cleanup is done.
  • Use the right disinfectant, correctly. Read the label for what the product is approved against and its contact time. Spraying and wiping right away does not disinfect. See cleaning vs. disinfecting.
  • Focus on high-touch surfaces. Door handles, restrooms, sinks, toys, tables and shared equipment.
  • Protect your crew. Cleaning staff need gloves, eye protection and training before they handle a bodily-fluid cleanup.
  • Keep restroom tools separate. Color-coded cloths and mops keep contamination from traveling.
