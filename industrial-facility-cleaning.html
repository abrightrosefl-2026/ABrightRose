Industrial Facility Cleaning: What to Expect and Cost

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Industrial cleaning is usually split between plant staff, specialized contractors and a janitorial vendor for offices and restrooms.
  • Safety training, site rules and chemical or hazardous material boundaries are central.
  • Cost depends on size, soil type, equipment needs, shift timing and risk.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Industrial facilities have different hazards and different cleaning needs than offices. Expect more safety planning and a clearer division of responsibilities.

What the work can include

  • Office, break room, locker room and restroom cleaning
  • Floor sweeping and scrubbing on production and warehouse floors
  • Dust and debris control on equipment and overhead surfaces
  • Spill response for non-hazardous materials, within agreed limits
  • Waste and recycling support
  • Periodic deep cleaning during shutdowns

What is usually specialized

  • Hazardous material cleanup
  • Confined space cleaning
  • Industrial vacuuming of combustible dust
  • Equipment cleaning that requires lockout and tagout
  • Pressure washing and degreasing of heavy equipment

These need contractors with specific training, equipment and insurance.

Safety and compliance

Facilities and contractors have responsibilities under OSHA, including hazard communication, personal protective equipment, lockout/tagout where machinery is involved and site-specific safety programs. Expect the vendor to attend site safety orientation and follow your rules. Ask for their safety record and training documentation.

What drives cost

  • Square footage and the type of floors
  • Level of soiling and the type of material
  • Equipment required (scrubbers, industrial vacuums, lifts)
  • Hazard level and required training
  • Shift timing, including nights, weekends or shutdowns
  • Insurance requirements

We are not giving a price range, because it varies too much by site. Ask for a walkthrough-based quote with a detailed scope.

How to choose a vendor

  • Experience with similar facilities and references
  • Safety program and training records
  • Insurance appropriate for the risk, confirmed by certificate
  • Equipment list
  • Clear boundaries on hazardous materials
  • Documentation of work completed

Clear boundaries prevent problems

Write down which areas, equipment and materials are included and which are not. Identify who responds to spills, and how incidents are reported.
