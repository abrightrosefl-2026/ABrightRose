Cleaning Chemicals You Should Never Mix: A Workplace Safety Guide

Published by the ABR team · Updated November 2026

Key takeaways
  • Never mix bleach with ammonia or with acids such as vinegar. Both combinations create toxic gas.
  • Use one product at a time, follow the label, ventilate, and rinse before switching products.
  • Keep safety data sheets handy and train anyone who handles chemicals.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Most cleaning accidents with chemicals happen the same way: someone thinks two cleaners will work better together. In a workplace, where more than one person may use the same supply closet, the risk is higher. This guide covers the combinations to avoid and the habits that prevent them.

The combinations to avoid

  • Bleach + ammonia (including some glass cleaners and urine) creates chloramine gas, which irritates the nose, throat and lungs.
  • Bleach + acids (including vinegar and many bathroom or toilet-bowl cleaners) creates chlorine gas, which is corrosive to the eyes, nose and throat.

Low exposure can cause burning, coughing and irritation. Higher or longer exposure can cause shortness of breath and serious lung injury, and people with existing lung conditions are at higher risk. This is general safety information from poison-control guidance, linked below.

Other combinations can also be unsafe or simply useless. Do not mix any two cleaning products unless the manufacturer says it is safe.

Safe habits for any workplace

  1. Use one product at a time. Rinse the surface with water before using a different product.
  2. Read the label. It states dilution, contact time, surfaces, ventilation and protective equipment.
  3. Never "top off" or refill a bottle with a different product. Keep products in their original containers, or in properly labeled secondary containers.
  4. Ventilate. Open doors, run exhaust fans and avoid closed, small rooms such as restrooms and storage closets.
  5. Wear the right protection, usually gloves and eye protection for concentrates and disinfectants.
  6. Keep safety data sheets (SDS) accessible for every product on site.
  7. Store products separately. Keep bleach and acids/ammonia-based products apart, away from heat, and out of reach of the public.

If someone is exposed

Move the person to fresh air right away. If breathing problems continue or get worse, call 911. For advice, contact Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 in the US. Tell them which products were involved.

Why this matters when you hire a cleaner

A trained crew uses a defined product list, labeled containers and written procedures, and knows which products never go together. When you interview a company, ask what chemicals they bring, how they are stored and labeled, and how their staff are trained.
