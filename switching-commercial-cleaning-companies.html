How to Transition to a New Commercial Cleaning Company

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Read your current contract first and give notice the way it requires.
  • Collect keys, fobs and codes, and change access when the old vendor leaves.
  • Plan a clean handoff and a 30-day review with the new company.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Switching is easier than most people expect when you plan it. The goal is no gap in service and no gap in security.

Step 1: Read the current contract

Find the term, the notice period and any termination fees. Note whether it auto-renews. Give notice in the way the contract requires, usually in writing. Ask your attorney if anything is unclear.

Step 2: Choose the replacement first

Do not cancel before you have a signed agreement with the new company. See how to choose a commercial cleaning company. Ask the new company what they need from you to start.

Step 3: Document the baseline

Walk the building with the new vendor and take photos of floors, restrooms, glass and any existing damage. This protects both sides and sets expectations.

Step 4: Share the scope and site rules

  • Written scope and checklist
  • Alarm and access instructions
  • Areas that are off limits
  • Supply locations and storage rules
  • Contacts for emergencies and maintenance issues
  • Any health, safety or licensing rules for your facility

Step 5: Control access

  • List every key, fob, badge and alarm code the old vendor had
  • Collect keys and fobs on the last day
  • Change alarm codes and deactivate old credentials
  • Give the new vendor unique access

Step 6: Plan the handoff

If possible, allow a short overlap or a single deep clean before the new vendor starts, so they begin from a clean baseline. Confirm the start date in writing.

Step 7: Review in the first 30 days

Schedule a check-in after the first week and again at 30 days. Share feedback early. Use a checklist, and ask for fixes within a set window.

Be professional with the outgoing vendor. Pay final invoices, return their equipment and keep communication factual. It protects you if there is a dispute about damage or missing items.

Common mistakes

  • Cancelling before the replacement is confirmed
  • Forgetting to change alarm codes
  • Not documenting the starting condition
  • Skipping the 30-day review
