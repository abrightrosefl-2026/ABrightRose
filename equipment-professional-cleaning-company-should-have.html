What Cleaning Equipment Should a Professional Company Have?

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Professional crews bring their own commercial-grade equipment, not your vacuum.
  • Look for color-coded tools, microfiber, HEPA filtration, wet floor signs and labeled chemicals.
  • Equipment that is clean and maintained is a good sign of a well-run company.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

You can learn a lot from what a crew carries into your building. Professional equipment is a sign of professional practice.

The essentials

  • Commercial vacuums. Upright or backpack vacuums with strong filtration. HEPA filters trap fine dust and are valuable in medical offices, daycares and anywhere with sensitive occupants.
  • Microfiber cloths and mops. They pick up more soil with less product and can be laundered and reused.
  • Color-coded tools. Different colors for restrooms, general surfaces, glass and kitchens help prevent cross-contamination. See disinfection basics.
  • Wet floor signs. Used before mopping starts and kept until floors are dry.
  • Janitor cart or caddy. Organized, with separated clean and dirty supplies.
  • Mop buckets with wringers and fresh mop heads.
  • Restroom-only tools. Dedicated brushes, cloths and mops that never leave the restroom.
  • Labeled spray bottles. Every secondary container should be labeled with the product name and hazards, as OSHA's Hazard Communication standard requires.
  • Dispensing system. Proportioner or measured dilution to avoid guessing.
  • Personal protective equipment. Gloves, eye protection and non-slip shoes.

For larger or specialized jobs

  • Floor scrubbers and burnishers
  • Carpet extractors
  • Ladders and extension poles for high dusting
  • Wet-dry vacuums for spills

What to ask a vendor

  1. What equipment will crews bring to our building?
  2. Do you use HEPA-filtered vacuums?
  3. Are cloths and mops color-coded, and how are they laundered?
  4. How are chemicals diluted and labeled?
  5. Do you maintain and replace equipment on a schedule?

What to look at during a visit

  • Is the cart clean and organized?
  • Are bottles labeled?
  • Do the vacuums look maintained?
  • Does the crew use signs and PPE?
Simple test: If a crew shows up with unlabeled bottles, one dirty rag for everything and a borrowed vacuum, expect the same level of care in the work.
