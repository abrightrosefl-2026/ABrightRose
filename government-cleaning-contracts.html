How to Obtain Government Cleaning Contracts: A Strategic Guide for Commercial Cleaners

Government cleaning contracts represent a significant opportunity for commercial cleaning companies seeking stable, long-term revenue streams. Unlike private sector contracts, government procurement offers predictable budgets, consistent payment terms, and the potential to scale your cleaning operations substantially. This guide walks you through the pathways to securing federal, state, and local government cleaning contracts.

🌟 Why Government Contracts Matter: Government entities account for billions in annual cleaning services spending. Contracts typically include multi-year terms, automatic renewals, and payment schedules that improve cash flow—making them ideal for growth-focused cleaning companies.

1. Understanding Government Contract Types

Federal GSA Contracts (Schedule 84)

The General Services Administration (GSA) maintains the largest contract vehicle for commercial cleaning services. GSA Schedule 84 is specifically designed for facility maintenance and cleaning contractors. Key advantages include:

  • Open to federal agencies nationwide – Your services become available to every federal facility
  • Simplified ordering process – Agencies can order directly without competitive bidding
  • Multi-year term – Typically 5 years with optional renewals up to 20 years total
  • Competitive pricing requirements – Discounts applied to commercial rates

Federal IDIQ Contracts (Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity)

IDIQ contracts establish a framework agreement where agencies order services as needed. These contracts often have minimum and maximum dollar amounts but provide exceptional stability once awarded.

State and Local Government Contracts

State, county, and municipal governments operate their own procurement systems. These contracts often have shorter award timelines and fewer technical requirements than federal contracts, making them an excellent starting point for companies new to government work.

3. Step-by-Step Path to Federal GSA Contracts

Step 1: Register Your Business

  • Obtain a DUNS Number (www.dnb.com) – typically free for small businesses
  • Register with SAM.gov and create an account – critical for all federal bidding
  • Complete the Online Representation and Certifications Application (ORCA)

Step 2: Prepare Your GSA Contract Proposal

Your proposal must include:

  • Labor rates by job classification (floor cleaner, supervisor, etc.)
  • Commercial pricing baseline – GSA requires discounts from your standard rates
  • Scope of services – detail what's included (vacuuming, mopping, restrooms, etc.)
  • Equipment and products list – quality, safety, and EPA certifications
  • Management approach – your systems for quality assurance and customer service
  • References – current clients (government contracts preferred but private sector acceptable)

Step 3: Submit Through eOffer or GSA's e-Buy System

The GSA provides the eOffer platform for submission. Allow 4–6 months for review and award decisions. GSA contracts typically follow a streamlined approval process once you're in the system.

Step 4: Maintain Compliance

Once awarded, you'll need to:

  • Update your labor rates annually (no more than 10% increase without justification)
  • Respond to federal agency orders within agreed timeframes
  • Maintain insurance and certifications throughout the contract term
  • Comply with prevailing wage requirements if applicable

4. State and Local Government Procurement

Where to Find Opportunities

  • FedBizOpps.gov – Federal contracts and some state/local listings

  • County/Municipal Websites – Government procurement sections often list active RFPs
  • Secondary platforms – ProcureNet, VendorRegistration.com

6. Challenges and How to Overcome Them

Challenge: Prevailing Wage Requirements

Some government contracts require you to pay prevailing wages (often higher than market rates). Solution: Research requirements upfront, adjust your pricing accordingly, and seek contracts that don't mandate prevailing wages if margin impact is too high.

Challenge: Bonding Costs

Performance bonds can cost 1–3% of contract value. Solution: Negotiate bonding costs into your pricing, or start with contracts below bonding thresholds ($100K) and scale upward as your business grows.

Challenge: Payment Processing Timeline

Government agencies may have 30–60 day payment cycles. Solution: Build this into your cash flow planning and consider invoicing systems designed for government contracts (e.g., invoice tracking platforms).
