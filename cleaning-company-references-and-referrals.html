Customer References and Referrals: How to Vet a Cleaning Company

Published by the ABR team · Updated October 2026

Key takeaways
  • Ask for references from facilities like yours, and actually call them.
  • Good questions focus on reliability, communication and how problems were handled.
  • A referral from someone you trust is helpful, but still check insurance and scope.
Published by A Bright Rose Cleaning Services, a commercial-only cleaning company in West Palm Beach, FL serving Palm Beach County.

Online reviews are a start. References and referrals tell you how a company really behaves once the contract is signed.

Where to get them

  • Provided references. Ask each company for two or three current clients, ideally with a similar building, size and schedule.
  • Referrals. Ask peers, your property manager, your association board or neighboring practices who they use and why.
  • Long-term clients. A client who has stayed several years tells you more than a brand-new one.

Questions to ask a reference

  1. How long have they cleaned for you?
  2. Do they arrive on time and finish the scope every visit?
  3. How do they handle a missed task or a complaint? How quickly?
  4. Has the crew been stable, or does it change often?
  5. Have you had any problems with security, damage or billing?
  6. Would you hire them again?

How to read the answers

  • Specific answers are good. "They fixed it the same day" beats "they're fine."
  • Hesitation is a signal. If a reference avoids a question, follow up.
  • Look for patterns. One late visit happens. Repeated issues do not.

If a company will not give references

Be careful. Some companies have confidentiality rules with clients and can offer a short letter or a call instead. Ask. If they offer nothing at all, treat it as a warning.

Do not skip the basics

A good referral does not replace verifying credentials such as insurance, or putting the scope in writing. Use references together with a structured interview.
